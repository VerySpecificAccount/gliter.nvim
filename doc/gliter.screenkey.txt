1. gliter.screenkey                        *gliter.screenkey-gliter.screenkey*

Shows the keys you press, as you press them: a small float above the cmdline,
the newest key highlighted, the rest fading away a moment after the last press.
For screencasts, demos and pair sessions — the audience sees the keys, not
your fingers.

>lua
    require('gliter').setup { screenkey = {} }
    -- or on its own:
    require('gliter.screenkey').setup {}
<

`:GliterScreenkey` toggles it. The module is opt-in like every gliter module,
so naming it in `setup()` is the on switch.

The key list comes from `vim.on_key` — the keys as they are typed, before any
mapping eats them — and is only ever shown: nothing is intercepted, nothing
is replayed. Keys are shown in key notation (`<C-n>`, `<Leader>`, `gg`), mouse
and wheel events stay out (hover noise), and a long burst (a paste) collapses
instead of flooding the display.


OPTIONS                            *gliter.screenkey-gliter.screenkey-options*

>lua
    require('gliter.screenkey').setup {
      timeout = 2000,   -- ms of quiet before the display clears
      max_keys = 10,    -- how many keys stay on screen
      border = 'none',
      row = nil,        -- 0-based screen row; nil = just above the cmdline
      col = nil,        -- 0-based screen column; nil = centered
    }
<

STOLEN FROM                    *gliter.screenkey-gliter.screenkey-stolen-from*

An Emacs idea with an X11 name. Emacs has shown your keys since the beginning
— `echo-keystrokes`, the built-in variable that echoes an unfinished key
sequence after a pause, and `view-lossage` (`C-h l`), which shows the last
keystrokes you typed, both built-ins from the 1980s — and showkey.el
<https://www.emacswiki.org/emacs/ShowKey> (Drew Adams, 2015) made the showing
live: `showkey-tooltip-mode` and `showkey-log-mode`, the keys you use as you
use them. screenkey.nvim <https://github.com/NStefan002/screenkey.nvim> (2024)
moved the idea into a Neovim float, under the name of the X11 `screenkey` tool
that drew keystrokes over the screen for recordings. gliter.screenkey is the
light version: one float, two colors, a timer.
