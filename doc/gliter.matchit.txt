1. gliter.matchit                              *gliter.matchit-gliter.matchit*

`%` for keywords. The key you gave the matchit job — `M` by default, the key
you rebound `%` to — jumps between the words that bracket a block: `if` and
`end` in Lua, `function` and `endfunction` in Vim script, `fi` closing
shell’s `if`, `#endif` closing `#ifdef`, `<div>` and `</div>` — with the
nesting counted, exactly the way `%` counts brackets.

>lua
    require('gliter').setup { matchit = {} }
    -- or on its own:
    require('gliter.matchit').setup {}
<


THE WALK                              *gliter.matchit-gliter.matchit-the-walk*

The chains, per filetype, are three Vim regexes — openers (`if`, `for`,
`function`, …), closers (`end`, `endif`, `fi`, …), middles (`else`,
`elseif`, `catch`, …) — and the walks are bracket walks:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Key   On an opener or a middle (if, elseif,    On a closer (end, </div>,
        else, …)                                 …)
  ----- ---------------------------------------- --------------------------
  M     its matching closer, nesting counted —   its matching opener
        if ↔ end, else → the end

  gM    the outer level: on a middle, its        its matching opener
        matching opener (else → the if); on an
        opener, out of the block — the previous
        member of the enclosing chain (the
        elseif/else this group sits after), or
        the enclosing opener itself: the outer
        level’s group/match_word

  gm    the chain’s next member, the middles     back to the chain’s opener
        included — if → elseif → else → end —    (end → if: the wrap)
        and from the end back to its if: the
        cycle wraps, one member per press

  ]M    the next group’s opener at the cursor’s  the same: the next group’s
        level — while 1<-if 2->function(3: from  opener
        if 2 to the function(3 of the group
        after it, the pair’s own edges skipped,
        middles never landed on

  [M    the previous group’s opener at the       the same: the previous
        cursor’s level — from if 2 back to the   group’s opener
        while 1 of the group before it
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
So `M` and `gM` differ only on the middle words and the opener — on `else`,
`M` goes to the end of the block and `gM` back to its start; on an `if`, `gM`
steps out of the block to the outer level’s match_word. `gm` walks every
member of the chain one by one, the middle words included. `]M`/`[M` move along
the groups of one level — the sibling blocks — and land on their
beginnings, the openers (`while`, `if`, `function`, never `elseif`/`else`); the
enclosing group’s edge, one level up, ends the walk with the cursor left
where it was.

>lua
    while 1 do          --  [M from the `if 2` below lands HERE (its group's opener)
    end
    if 2 then           --  the cursor's group: [M back, ]M ahead
    end
    function(3)         --  ]M from the `if 2` above lands HERE
    end
<

In Visual mode the selection extends to the match; after an operator (`dM`,
`cM`) the whole match word is included, the way `d%` includes both brackets.

On a word that is none of the chain — or in a filetype with no chains — the
key falls back to plain `%`: brackets, and everything `'matchpairs'` holds. A
count keeps Vim’s own `[count]%` (go to N% of the file).

**If M does not jump for you, look for a remap of your own.** A
`vim.keymap.set('n', 'M', '%')` in your config — the natural thing to have
done before installing gliter — runs after gliter’s `setup()` and wins over
this module’s `M`. Remove it (plain `%` already does `%`), or pick another
key with `keys.match` below. `:verbose nmap M` names the file the winning
mapping came from.

HTML tags: from `<div ...>` to the matching `</div>` and back, counting nested
`<div>`s of the same name; self-closing tags (`<br/>`) count for nothing; the
cursor can be anywhere inside the tag. `gm` on a tag pair is `M` (edge to
edge); `]M`/`[M` walk the neighboring pairs of the same name the same way.


THE CHAINS                          *gliter.matchit-gliter.matchit-the-chains*

Three Vim regexes per filetype — `opens`, `middles`, `closes` — shipped for
**lua, vim, sh/bash/zsh, c/cpp** (the C preprocessor pairs, plus `/*` and
`*/`). Python has no closing keywords to chain and is left to the fallback. Add
your own, or override a built-in, through `words`:

>lua
    require('gliter.matchit').setup {
      words = {
        ruby = {
          opens  = [[\<\%(def\|class\|module\|begin\|case\)\>]],
          middles = [[\<\%(rescue\|ensure\|else\|elsif\|when\)\>]],
          closes = [[\<end\>]],
        },
      },
    }
<

In netrw buffers `M` is netrw’s own (gliter.netrw’s move-all mark, a
buffer-local mapping that wins there); everywhere else it is this module’s.


OPTIONS                                *gliter.matchit-gliter.matchit-options*

>lua
    require('gliter.matchit').setup {
      keys = {
        match = 'M',   -- n/x/o: jump to the matching keyword / tag / bracket
        back  = 'gM',  -- n/x/o: the other way — on an opener, out to the
                       --   outer level's group/match_word
        member = 'gm', -- n/x/o: the chain's next member, middles included
                       --   (if → elseif → else → end → if …, wrapping)
        next_group = ']M', -- n/x/o: the next group's opener at this level
        prev_group = '[M', -- n/x/o: the previous group's opener at this level
      },
      words = {},               -- your chains, merged over the built-ins
      tags = { 'html', 'xhtml', 'xml' }, -- filetypes with <tag> matching
    }
<


STOLEN FROM                        *gliter.matchit-gliter.matchit-stolen-from*

matchit.vim
<https://github.com/vim/vim/blob/master/runtime/pack/dist/opt/matchit/plugin/matchit.vim>
— Raul Segura Acevedo’s 1996 script, grown by Benji Fisher — is the
reference, and ships with Vim and Neovim as an optional plugin (`:packadd
matchit`). It does more: backreferences, comment skipping, `%` from anywhere on
the line, `[%`/`]%`, `i%`. gliter.matchit is the walk and nothing more. **For
the rest, :packadd matchit or vim-matchup.**
