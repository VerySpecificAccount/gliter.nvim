1. gliter.move                                       *gliter.move-gliter.move*

A visual move mode: one key turns the current Visual selection into a thing you
carry around the buffer (Neovim 0.10+). Enabled on `<C-m>` — charwise,
linewise and blockwise selections alike — the keys stop extending the
selection and start MOVING it, nvim-gomove’s engine doing the moving. A
session that ends the wrong way takes its changes back in ONE step.

>lua
    require('gliter').setup { move = {} }
    -- or on its own:
    require('gliter.move').setup {}
<


WHAT IT DOES                            *gliter.move-gliter.move-what-it-does*

Press `<C-m>` on a selection and a hint line appears — the mode is on:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Key                  What moves
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  h j k l              the gomove moves — [count] works (3j is three
                       rows)

  0                    the line’s left edge: the selection slides flush
                       against it (a line selection dedents fully)

  $                    the line’s right edge: the selection slides flush
                       against the end of the longest row it covers

  gg G                 the buffer’s edges: the lines/the block flush to
                       the top (gg) or bottom (G); {count}gg lands the
                       top row on line {count}

  f{char}              slides the selection toward the char — the carry
                       lands just past it, the whole char carried intact;
                       [count] is the Nth occurrence

  t{char}              the same, stopping one short of that

  F{char}              the backward twin: slides the selection back
                       toward the char

  T{char}              the same, stopping one short of it

  w e b                the word motions, LINE-WISE: the carry is the
                       slide — a word never takes the selection to
                       another row. FORWARD is Vim’s own direction, left
                       to right, and the walk is ROW-FIRST: the words
                       of the line the CURSOR is on — the first line,
                       where the cursor was — are walked before any
                       other’s, and a selection that goes VERTICAL —
                       several rows, V or <C-v> alike — moves forward by
                       the words in the first line, then after reaching
                       its end goes on to the NEXT BIGGEST LINE: the
                       next row that still reaches past the edge, the
                       walk continuing from there (visual mode’s own
                       expanding-to-the-right shape), walking all the
                       way to the RIGHT END of the LONGEST LINE covered
                       — past it w is dead — w landing the selection’s
                       right edge on the next word’s first character, e
                       on the next word-end, one word a press. b is the
                       backward twin: the left edge lands on the previous word’s
                       first, right to left — with a FLOOR: the beginning
                       of the line, b keeps moving back until column 1,
                       all the way to 0, and past it is dead. On a line
                       selection (V) the carry is the COLUMN shift: every
                       line moves together by exact columns, the walking
                       edge the cursor row’s own first word — and b’s
                       floor is the FULL dedent, every line to its own
                       beginning. [count] is the Nth word. A word is
                       Vim’s own ('iskeyword'), the way w reads one

  W E B                the WORD twins of w/e/b: Vim’s WORDs — non-blank
                       runs, punctuation riding along — the same
                       line-wise carry (W the right edge on the next
                       WORD’s first character, E its end, B the left edge
                       on the previous WORD’s first), the same
                       cursor’s-line world, the same b-to-0 floor

  1..9                 a count for the next move

  o / O                not a move: the cursor walks to another corner of
                       the selection — which corner is the moving one is
                       now that one
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
What each move does per selection kind — gomove’s own truth:

- **charwise, one line** — `j`/`k` lift the span onto the neighbor row
    at the same column; `h`/`l` slide it through the line, swapping with
    the text passed over (`abXYcd` with `XY` selected slides right to
    `abcXYd`).
- **charwise, several lines** — from the line’s left edge, whole rows
    move as a block of lines, exactly the way `V` moves; otherwise the
    RECTANGLE of the selection’s corners moves as a block: cut out of its
    rows, re-inserted a block of rows away at the same column — the
    landing rows shift right, nothing is overwritten.
- **linewise (V)** — the lines move as a block; `h`/`l` indent and
    unindent one shiftwidth per press (the classic drag).
- **blockwise (<C-v>)** — `j`/`k` cut the block out and re-INSERT it
    a block of rows away at the same column; `h`/`l` slide the block
    THROUGH each row, short rows padded where the block needs room to
    land (the hug).

The goto motions (`0 $ gg G f t F T w e b W E B`) carry the selection as the
same two carries `hjkl` speak — rows, then columns. What the selection passes
over shifts behind it, order intact. A pending `f`/`t`/`F`/`T` char is let go
with `<Esc>` — the mode stays. `0` `$` `f` `t` `F` `T` are dead on a line
selection (`0` dedents it fully, and the word motions carry it by columns —
see below); `w`/`e`/`b`/ `W`/`E`/`B` read the CURSOR’S OWN LINE alone — all
the way to the right to that line’s end, `b` back to the line’s own
beginning — and never take the selection to another row; `gg`/`G` work on
every shape. The SCREEN cursor stays in the buffer for the whole ride — on
the selection, on the jump — never on the message line at the bottom of the
screen under the hint (0.11+; an older nvim shows it there, the way a
`getchar()` always has).


FROM NORMAL MODE: THE <C-M> MODE ~

`<C-m>` in normal mode is a MODE — a getchar() loop, the visual session’s
own shape, that stays until it is left:

- **<C-m>{C-h C-j C-k C-l}** — TRANSPOSE WORDS, Emacs’
    `transpose-words` with Vim’s own words (`'iskeyword'` decides what a
    word is, the way `w` does), in the DIRECTION the key names — the
    Ctrl is ON, the way the exchange’s aim keys spell theirs: `C-m C-l`
    with the word after the cursor’s (Emacs’ own `M-t`), `C-m C-h` with
    the one before, `C-m C-j` with the word below, `C-m C-k` with the
    word above — the word whose span sits nearest the cursor word’s
    column, blank lines crossed the way `w` crosses them. The plain
    `h j k l o i` are REPLACED, not aliased — `C-m C-o` is not the same
    as `C-m o` — a plain letter is a key the mode does not know. In
    `foo(bar, baz)` the cursor on `bar` and `<C-m>C-l` is
    `foo(baz, bar)` — the args
    trade places, the punctuation does not move, everything around both
    words stays exactly what it was. On a gap — whitespace, a comma —
    the words AROUND it are the pair (Emacs’ rule), `C-h` and `C-l`
    reading the same pair from either side. The trade is made ONCE and
    the mode is done with it — a second trade takes another `<C-m>`
    (the old `<C-t>{direction}` spelling works the same). `[count]`
    bubbles the word along in that direction, the cursor riding it.
- **<C-m>C-o / <C-m>C-i** — THE JUMPS, THIS FILE’S TRAIL ONLY:
    Vim’s own jump list (your `gg`, `G`, marks, `%` — everything that
    jumps feeds it) walked with only the current file’s entries applied —
    a jump that went to another file is skipped, never taken, so a
    session of file1 > file2 > file1 walks file1’s positions alone.
    `C-o` steps back, `C-i` forward — the cursor LANDS on each location,
    `[count]` locations at a time, and every press keeps walking — the
    keys mix freely inside one `<C-m>`
    (`<C-m>C-i C-o C-o` steps forward one, back two); the first
    step back leaves the newest position remembered the native way, so
    a step forward returns to it. `jumps.scope = 'own'` walks a
    separate list per file instead — Vim’s jump records bucketed per
    file, each list outliving Vim’s window-shared list and untouched by
    what the other files do.

`[count]` works typed before `<C-m>` or between the mode’s keys. `<Esc>` and
`<C-c>` leave the mode quietly; anything else — a letter, an operator — is
let go with a short notice. Note that `<C-m>` and `<CR>` are the same key on
most terminals; buffers whose `<CR>` is their own (netrw’s open, the
quickfix’s select, help’s follow) never see the mode — their `<CR>` maps
are buffer-local and a local map beats a global one — and `keys.normal =
false` gives Enter back everywhere.


HOW IT ENDS — KEEP, UNDO, EXCHANGE *gliter.move-gliter.move-how-it-ends-—-keep,-undo,-exchange*

- `<C-t>` — KEEP: the moves stay, the selection is dropped, back to
    normal mode on what moved (`<C-m><C-t>`, the prefix spelling, still
    works inside the mode).
- `<Esc>` / `<C-c>` — UNDO: the buffer goes back to the state it had
    when the mode was entered — the undo sequence number the session
    started from is remembered, and the cancellation is one
    `:undo {seq}` to it, exact from any depth — and the cursor returns
    to where the mode was entered. Any key the mode does not know — an
    operator, a mode change, anything that is not a move — does the
    same: leaving is a cancellation, not a confirmation.
- `<C-x>` — EXCHANGE, entirely inside the mode: the first press marks
    the selection as the first area of an exchange — it stays
    highlighted and the mode exits to normal mode for selecting the
    second area. A move made first is UNDONE by the press: the
    exchange trades what was SELECTED, not where a session carried it,
    so the buffer goes back to the state it had at entry and the
    selection back to where it started before anything is marked. The
    second `<C-x>` — pressed in the mode again
    (`C-m`, moves, `<C-x>`), on a plain Visual selection, or from
    normal mode — swaps the two areas, whatever each side selected;
    moves made before it are undone the same
    way. From normal mode the four DIRECTION keys are spelled with the
    Ctrl on: `C-x C-h/j/k/l` swaps with the word before, the word
    after, the line below, the line above — the mode’s own directions,
    read off the live cursor. The plain `h j k l` after `C-x` are free
    — they just move, an accidental `hjkl` never swaps a sliver — and
    every other motion aims natively: `C-x w`, `C-x f{char}`, `C-x $`,
    `C-x gg` and the rest, the motion’s own span.

A MIXED pair swaps too — a block against a word, a span or a line,
    whatever each side selected: the word lands in the rectangle’s
    top-left (the word right on the block’s own top row trades inside
    that row, the span path’s own shape), and the rectangle’s rows are
    carried into the word’s place — a span’s row count follows the
    text, the row splitting under the pieces; a line range’s rows are
    the text; the rectangle’s other rows keep the blank the cut
    leaves. A marked block highlights the RECTANGLE it selected — one
    mark per row, each that row’s own slice — never the diagonal a
    single mark would light (a block that reads as turned into a
    plain Visual selection the moment `<C-x>` is pressed on it).

A pending exchange does not wait forever: `<Esc>` and `<C-c>` cancel it (normal
or Visual mode — abandoning the second selection counts), and so does
ANYTHING that touches the buffer or leaves the normal/Visual pair — an edit,
a paste, a delete, one of your own move-line maps, a jump into insert. Motions
never cancel it: `C-x` and the aim keys are how the second area is picked. The
marked area is a snapshot; the moment something else changes the text, the wait
is over and the mark is cleared with a short notice.

Normal mode’s own `<C-x>` (the decrement) is never taken: the exchange lives
inside the mode and on the Visual-mode key, and the aim form is a buffer-local
shadow that exists only while an exchange is waiting — `C-x C-h/j/k/l` for
the directions, `C-x{motion}` for any motion.


ONE UNDO                                    *gliter.move-gliter.move-one-undo*

The moves are one undo entry: every move after the first is `:undojoin`-ed onto
it, so a cancellation is a single `undo` — and `u` after a KEPT session takes
the whole session back too.


THE CARRY AS AN API: CARRY_TO *gliter.move-gliter.move-the-carry-as-an-api:-carry_to*

The goto motions’ engine is exposed for other plugins to aim — a flash.nvim
jump, a treehopper node, your own maps:

>lua
    require('gliter.move').carry_to({ row, col }, region?)
<

`{ row, col }` is 1-based byte columns; `region` is the selection to carry (`{
mode = 'v'|'V'|'\22', r1, c1, r2, c2 }`), the live Visual selection when left
out. The selection is left selected where it lands, and the carry is one undo
entry.

With flash.nvim <https://github.com/folke/flash.nvim> — `S` on a selection
picks a label, the selection lands where it points (flash’s positions are
0-based, `carry_to`’s are 1-based):

>lua
    vim.keymap.set('x', 'S', function()
      local v1, v2 = vim.fn.getpos('v'), vim.fn.getpos('.')
      local kind = vim.fn.visualmode()
      require('flash').jump({
        action = function(match)
          require('gliter.move').carry_to({ match.pos[1] + 1, match.pos[2] + 1 }, {
            mode = kind, r1 = v1[2], c1 = v1[3], r2 = v2[2], c2 = v2[3],
          })
        end,
      })
    end)
<

The selection is read BEFORE flash runs (flash’s label UI leaves Visual mode)
and handed to `carry_to` as the explicit region.


OPTIONS                                      *gliter.move-gliter.move-options*

>lua
    require('gliter.move').setup {
      keys = {
        mode = '<C-m>',     -- x: enter the move mode on the selection
        normal = '<C-m>',   -- n: the mode — C-h C-j C-k C-l transpose
                            --    the adjacent words in that direction
                            --    (once), C-o/C-i walk this file's jumps,
                            --    repeatable inside one <C-m> — the plain
                            --    h j k l o i are replaced, not aliased
                            --    (false keeps Enter)
        confirm = '<C-t>',  -- in-mode: keep the moves
        exchange = '<C-x>', -- in-mode / x: mark the area, then swap
                            --    (a pending wait also takes
                            --    <C-x>{motion} from normal mode)
      },
      hint = true,            -- echo the keys while the mode runs
      reindent = false,       -- reindent lines after a vertical line move
      move_past_end_col = true, -- a block may slide past the end of the
                              -- shortest row it covers (the hug)
      jumps = {
        scope = 'file', -- 'file': C-m C-o/C-i walk Vim's own jump list,
                        -- only the current file's entries — a jump that
                        -- went to another file is skipped, never taken;
                        -- 'own': a separate jump list per file (Vim's
                        -- jump records, bucketed per file, each file's
                        -- list its own — a list outlives Vim's
                        -- window-shared list and survives the file's
                        -- closing, in memory for the session)
        max = 100,      -- 'own' only: each file's list depth
      },
    }
<

The gomove pair are that plugin’s own options, passed through: `reindent`
(defaults to `true` there, `false` here — a move moves, it does not reformat)
and `move_past_end_col` (defaults to `false` there, `true` here). The `jumps`
table shapes `<C-m><C-o>`/`<C-m><C-i>`: `scope = 'file'` (the default) keeps
Vim’s own jump list and walks it with only the current file’s entries
applied — the walk’s step itself is native (`C-o`/`C-i` fed with the
distance to the next entry of this file, foreign entries passed over, never
taken), so the cursor lands the way it always lands and the round trip back to
where the walk began is Vim’s own; `scope = 'own'` keeps instead a separate
list per file — every jump Vim records lands in its file’s own list, and a
file’s list keeps its place while you walk another file’s. The lists of
`scope = 'own'` remember where jumps happened, not how the text moved since —
positions are clamped to the file as it stands.


NOT THE WHOLE OF GOMOVE, NOT THE WHOLE OF ISWAP *gliter.move-gliter.move-not-the-whole-of-gomove,-not-the-whole-of-iswap*

Use booperlv/nvim-gomove <https://github.com/booperlv/nvim-gomove> for more:
duplicating while moving, folding handling, stack moves, the diagnostic floats.
For richer EXCHANGES than the two-area swap here — treesitter-aware swapping,
dragging across levels — use mizlan/iswap.nvim
<https://github.com/mizlan/iswap.nvim>.


STOLEN FROM                              *gliter.move-gliter.move-stolen-from*

The moving engine is booperlv/nvim-gomove’s (2021), ported into
`lua/gliter/move/gomove.lua` — the line moves (`:move`, the indent drag), the
block moves (the cut-and-paste rectangle, the virtualedit walk, the
trailing-whitespace bookkeeping), the fold-aware destination arithmetic. The
goto motions (`0 $ gg G f t F T w e b`) are gliter’s own, composed from
gomove’s carries. The features themselves are Emacs’: `transpose-lines` on
`C-x C-t` and `transpose-words` on `M-t` — the transpose family the GNU Emacs
manual has carried since the 1980s — swap the line under point (or the words
around it), and a numeric argument repeats the swap into a move. rejeep’s
drag-stuff (2010) turned the swap into a carry: `M-<up>`/`M-<down>` take the
line, or the region, along. tommcdo/vim-exchange (2013) is the two-press swap
(`cxx` … `cxx`). gliter.move folds the carry, the exchange and the transpose
into one mode with one undo.
