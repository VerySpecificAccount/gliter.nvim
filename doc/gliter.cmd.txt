1. gliter.cmd                                          *gliter.cmd-gliter.cmd*

readline’s keys on the command line Neovim already has, and fish’s ghost on
top of it. The built-in command line stays the command line: `:` is `:` and
`q:` is `q:`, every builtin key keeps working, Tab and S-Tab above all — the
wildmenu opens and walks exactly the way it always did. gliter.cmd adds the
keys readline has had since 1988 that the built-in line lacks, and draws the
history’s continuation dimmed after the line, fish’s autosuggestion (2005).
That is the whole module.

>lua
    require('gliter').setup { cmd = {} }
    -- or on its own:
    require('gliter.cmd').setup {}
<


THE KEYS                                      *gliter.cmd-gliter.cmd-the-keys*

What the built-in line already has readline-style is left alone: `C-w` kills
the word before the cursor, `C-u` to the beginning, `C-h` and BS delete, `C-r`
inserts a register (`C-r C-w`: the word under the cursor), `C-v` inserts
literally, `C-y` pastes the selection, `Up`/`Down` walk the history,
`Home`/`End`/`Left`/`Right` and `S-Left`/`S-Right` move the way they always
have. What readline spells and the built-in line does not, gliter.cmd adds —
each a cnoremap that edits the line through `setcmdline()`, the way
`c_CTRL-\_e` always did:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Key                         Action
  --------------------------- -------------------------------------------
  C-a                         to the beginning of the line — Home’s
                              motion (the built-in C-a, inserting every
                              match, waits the wildmenu out: Tab’s lot is
                              untouched)

  C-b                         back a character — Left’s motion (the
                              built-in C-b, to the beginning, was never
                              readline’s)

  C-f                         forward a character; at the end of the
                              line, the ghost taken

  C-e                         to the end of the line; at the end, the
                              ghost taken

  C-d                         delete the character at the cursor — Del’s
                              motion

  C-k                         kill to the end of the line

  C-t                         transpose the characters at the cursor — at
                              the end of the line the last two, the way
                              readline drags them

  M-b                         back a word — S-Left’s motion

  M-f                         forward a word — S-Right’s motion

  M-BS                        kill the word before the cursor

  M-d                         kill the word after the cursor
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
The keys whose built-in job differs keep a door behind them: `C-f`’s old job
of opening the command-line window is `q:`’s alone now (map `'cedit'` to
something else if you want it back from the line), and `C-k` still spells
digraphs in Insert mode — on the command line it is readline’s kill.

**Tab and S-Tab are not touched.** The wildmenu is the builtin completion it
always was, and while it is up its own keys go straight to it: `C-n`/`C-p` walk
the matches, `Left`/`Right` select them, `C-e` goes back to what was typed,
`C-y` accepts — the edit keys sleep while the menu is up (nothing happens;
`C-e` dismisses it, or keep walking). The built-in `C-n`/`C-p` outside the menu
— the newer/older history recall — keeps working too: whenever there is no
ghost to act on, `C-n` and `C-p` hand themselves back to the built-in keys.


THE GHOST                                    *gliter.cmd-gliter.cmd-the-ghost*

The newest history entry that continues the line, drawn dimmed after it in
`GliterCmdGhost` colors (Comment’s ink on the message area’s paper, a float
that never takes focus — `:hi GliterCmdGhost` to taste):

- fish’s autosuggestion: `:w` with `file` ghosted after it, and Enter
    runs `:w` — the ghost is never the line;
- `C-n`, or `C-f`/`C-e`/`Right` at the end of the line, takes it: the
    line becomes the entry, the cursor at its end, and the next echo
    ghosts after that;
- `C-p` cycles the echoes — the other entries that also continue the
    line, one older at a time, the text staying; `C-n` cycles them back,
    one newer at a time, and at the newest it takes the ghost (so one
    `C-p` to peek at the older echo and one `C-n` to return leaves the
    line exactly as it was); past the last one nothing is shown, and
    `C-p` is the built-in history recall again — as `C-n` is when there
    is no ghost at all;
- `C-y` and `M-l` take whichever echo is showing — the line becomes it,
    the cursor at its end. (`C-y`’s built-in job on the line, copying a
    modeless selection, is given up for it; while the wildmenu is up the
    menu keeps the key — its own accept.)
- the entries come from the line’s own history: `:` commands for the
    Ex line, `/` and `?` searches for the search line, `@` answers for
    `input()`’s prompts.

The other prompts are command lines too: `input()`’s — netrw’s `%<mark>`
regexp (with its live match count at the right end of the row), w3’s address
— carry the same keys and the same ghost, with their answers in the `@`
history (`Up`/`Down` walk them; histadd() feeds it). The `=` expression prompt
and friends have no history, so no ghost — the keys still edit them.


OPTIONS                                        *gliter.cmd-gliter.cmd-options*

>lua
    require('gliter.cmd').setup {
      ghost = true,       -- the history's continuation after the line;
                          -- false = the readline keys alone, C-n/C-p/Right
                          -- left to their built-in jobs
      max_history = 200,  -- how many history entries feed the ghost echoes
    }
<

`require('gliter.cmd').state()` reports the command line that is up — its
text, the echoes it could be, which one is showing and its ghost — for tests
and statuslines.


COMPATIBILITY                            *gliter.cmd-gliter.cmd-compatibility*

Neovim 0.10+ (`setcmdline()`, the CmdlineChanged autocommands, and
`nvim__redraw()` — a float painted while the command line is up is not
composited on its own, and the flush is what draws the ghost; on 0.9 the keys
and the take work, the ghost rides unseen). No other gliter module is needed;
netrw’s and w3’s prompts work without gliter.cmd set up — they are plain
`input()` then, with `Up`/`Down` walking the `@` history.
